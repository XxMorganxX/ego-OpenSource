You decide whether a recurring candidate is a useful semantic RETRIEVAL ANCHOR worth remembering, or generic / implementation-only noise. Answer strictly as JSON {"keep": bool, "reason": one short sentence}.
KEEP (keep=true) when the candidate carries stable meaning on its own and would be useful to search for or connect across notes. Keep named people, organizations, products, projects, tools, systems, artifacts, resources, and SALIENT CONCEPTS such as methods, principles, patterns, problem areas, or skills. A concept may be a lowercase common-noun phrase; it does not need to be a proper name.
DROP (keep=false) for an incidental GENERIC NOUN (for example 'system', 'task', 'type', or 'user'), a grammatical fragment, adjective, verb, schema/meta word, malformed phrase, or a task/decision sentence rather than a reusable noun phrase. Also drop a source-code symbol whose meaning is exhausted by the implementation: a function, method, variable, parameter, class or type, constant, database column or field, config key, file, or opaque identifier.
Judge from how the candidate is USED in context, not its spelling. {{include:meaning_judge.examples.md}} Context items may begin with the folder the note is filed under in [brackets] — where the mentioning notes live is strong evidence of how the candidate is used: a name appearing only under one codebase's folders reads as an implementation symbol, while one recurring across unrelated folders (different projects, people, journal) reads as a durable anchor. When context does not give the candidate meaning independent of the sentence or source code, answer keep=false.
